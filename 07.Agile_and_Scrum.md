# Agile and Scrum

## 1. What Is Agile?

Agile is an approach to software development in which software is built in small steps, with regular feedback and improvements.

Instead of waiting until the entire project is complete, teams deliver working software frequently.

**Example:** A team develops a shopping app by first building login, then product browsing, and later payment features.

## 2. Principles of Agile

- Deliver working software frequently.
- Welcome changing requirements.
- Communicate regularly with customers.
- Work together as a team.
- Improve the process continuously.
- Focus on customer satisfaction.

## 3. What Is Scrum?

Scrum is a framework used to apply Agile principles.

Work is divided into short periods called **Sprints**, during which the team aims to complete a set of tasks.

A Sprint commonly lasts **1–4 weeks**.

**Example:** A team spends two weeks developing and testing the login and registration features of an application.

## 4. Scrum Roles

### 1. Product Owner
Decides what is most important to build and manages the Product Backlog.

### 2. Scrum Master
Helps the team understand Scrum and removes obstacles that affect progress.

### 3. Developers
Build, test, and deliver the product increment during each Sprint.

## 5. Scrum Events

### 1. Sprint
A fixed-length period in which the team works toward a goal.

### 2. Sprint Planning
The team decides what work to do during the Sprint and how to approach it.

### 3. Daily Scrum
A short daily meeting for Developers to inspect progress and plan their next steps.

### 4. Sprint Review
The team and stakeholders inspect the results and discuss what to do next.

### 5. Sprint Retrospective
The team discusses what went well, what could improve, and how to work better.

## 6. Scrum Artifacts

### 1. Product Backlog
A prioritized list of work needed for the product.

### 2. Sprint Backlog
The selected backlog items and plan for the current Sprint.

### 3. Increment
The usable product result produced during a Sprint, together with previous increments.

## 7. Agile vs. Waterfall

| Agile | Waterfall |
|---|---|
| Work is done in small cycles. | Work generally follows sequential phases. |
| Handles changing requirements more easily. | Changes can be harder to manage later. |
| Working software is delivered frequently. | Delivery is often later in the process. |
| Regular feedback is encouraged. | Feedback may be less frequent during development. |

## 8. Advantages of Agile

- Adapts to changing requirements.
- Provides frequent feedback.
- Delivers useful features in small steps.
- Encourages teamwork.
- Helps teams improve continuously.

## 9. Key Takeaways

- Agile emphasizes flexibility, collaboration, and frequent delivery.
- Scrum is a framework for applying Agile principles.
- A Sprint is a fixed-length period of work.
- The Product Owner manages product priorities.
- The Scrum Master helps the team use Scrum effectively.
- Developers create the product increment.
- Scrum includes events and artifacts that help organize work.
